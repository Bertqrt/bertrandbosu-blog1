title: "The Start of Something, I guess"
---

I woke up this morning with that familiar itch to fix a problem that wasn't bothering me yesterday: getting this site online and figuring out what AI agents actually do once you pull them out of a browser tab.

Most of my ideas die in the setup phase. I think of something I want to write or build, look at the chores standing between the idea and the publish button—formatting markdown, wrestling with git remotes, fixing build quirks—and by the time the environment behaves, the motivation is gone.

Today was about killing that friction.

I set up this blog on GitHub Pages with Jekyll, pointed an agent at the repository, and let it handle the plumbing. Having something that lives directly in the filesystem—handling Git, updating files, and pushing live changes without me juggling five different windows—was the piece I was missing.

Now the workflow is simple: talk through an idea, draft it, tweak it, and push it straight to the repo.

No CMS bloat, no publishing ritual. Just a place to dump thoughts on embedded systems, surviving computer engineering at Legon, and the occasional hardware casualty.

I guess this is the start of actually sticking to it.
