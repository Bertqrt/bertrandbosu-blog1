I was about to sleep the other night and my thoughts were all over the place. I wasn't trying to come up with anything to post. I was just thinking about thinking, you know? Then a line from the theme song of an old cartoon I used to watch hit me:

> *Making mistakes is nothing to fear*

And I couldn't let it go, because it's true, and most of us live like it isn't.

## The part that actually costs us

Everyone has stuff sitting in their head. Someone they want to talk to. A skill they want to learn. Something new they want to take on. And then this little voice shows up: *what if I mess it up?*

Here's what I think about that voice. **The mistake itself usually isn't the problem. The fear of the mistake stops people before they even begin, and that's the real problem.** You don't fail at the thing you never started. You just never find out.

## Fear is bad at math

I'm an engineering student, so obviously careful is good. If you wire a circuit wrong, it's either you blow something up or the thing just doesn't work. That fear makes sense. But look at how we treat everything else:

We treat *"I might say something awkward to a stranger"* like it's the same size as *"I might fry my board."* One costs you a mildly awkward 10 seconds. The other costs you money or could actually hurt you. **It feels the same but the stakes are wildly different.**

You could even say this phenomenon is similar to debugging. Debugging is just making mistakes on purpose until something works. Nobody in my field thinks a bug means you're bad at it. So why does that stop applying the moment it's a conversation or a new skill?

## Two times I held back

**This website.** I saw it as a long task and was sure I'd make so many mistakes that I shouldn't even try. Now it's up and running, and I keep asking myself *why was I so scared?* It's kinda funny. And yes, I did make mistakes. I tried pushing the glass look too far, hated it, and reverted it. Cost me a bit of time and that was it. That's what I was afraid of.

**A conversation.** There was someone I wanted to talk to, but I was scared I'd mess up how I'd say things, so I held back. Then they came and started talking to me themselves. Suddenly all that fear looked ridiculous. Not dumb, just kind of funny. I'd been rehearsing for an audience that wasn't even grading me.

## But the fear doesn't leave

I'm not going to tell you the fear goes away, because it doesn't. We're all human, aren't we? It'll still show up every time.

What changed for me is realizing its size is a **bad estimate**. It's telling you the stakes are huge when they're usually tiny. So start anyway, and adjust as you go.

> Making mistakes is nothing to fear. Never starting is.
